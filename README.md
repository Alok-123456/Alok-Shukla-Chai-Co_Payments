# Alok-Shukla-Chai-Co_Payments
Based on your PowerPoint presentation, “Taking Chai &amp; Co Online: A Practical Plan for Accepting Payments on a New Website and App,” here is a professional description you can use for your capstone project submission, LinkedIn post, or project overview. It covers the project's objectives, payment methods, cost analysis, security, and inal recommend.
This capstone project, “Taking Chai & Co Online,” presents a payment strategy for a Bengaluru-based tea brand selling online. Its objective is a secure, affordable, and reliable checkout experience.

The business scenario assumes monthly online sales of ₹15 lakh, an average order value of ₹600, approximately 2,500 monthly orders, and 70% of orders coming from mobile devices. These figures highlight the importance of fast, mobile-friendly checkout.

The recommended strategy prioritises UPI and credit or debit cards, while offering netbanking and selected wallets. EMI and pay-later options can be considered later if demand justifies them. A branded hosted checkout supports a consistent brand experience and reduces the engineering and security effort required for a custom payment interface.

The project recommends starting with an authorised payment aggregator. One integration can support multiple payment methods and simplify refunds, reporting, and support. It compares illustrative transaction fees across UPI, cards, netbanking, and wallets. Under the assumptions used, estimated fees total ₹15,000 per month before GST on ₹15 lakh in sales. These figures are illustrative; actual charges must be confirmed through written provider quotations.

The payment flow explains how customers select products, complete checkout, authenticate through a bank or payment network, and receive confirmation before merchant settlement. The backend should verify signed provider notifications rather than trust a browser success screen. Order, payment, and settlement statuses should be tracked separately.

Operational recommendations include daily reconciliation of payment reports and bank payouts, investigation of unmatched transactions and refunds, and clear ownership of unresolved issues. Security measures include hosted payment fields, applicable card authentication, limits on repeated attempts, multi-factor authentication for administrator accounts, and never storing CVV or raw card details.

The project highlights merchant verification, customer privacy, clear refund and shipping policies, and applicable compliance requirements. It considers future UAE expansion without complicating the initial India-focused launch.

Success will be measured through payment success rates, mobile checkout conversion, payment costs, payout accuracy, fraud and disputes, and customer feedback. Overall, the recommendation is to launch simply, test the mobile payment journey, reconcile payments every working day, and optimise provider pricing and international readiness using real business data.
